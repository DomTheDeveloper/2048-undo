\section{Mass barriers and certified arrangements}
\label{sec:deductions}

The certificates can be used as reusable constructions, not merely as
endpoints of a search. This section resolves two arrangement questions
posed in the earlier draft, gives a second proof of the extremal bounds,
and derives policy-independent bounds for honest play. The new
probability arguments and arrangement certificates are not advertised as
Lean-formalised; the quantitative deadline lemma below is kernel-checked.

\subsection{Minimum-length full chains beyond corner snakes}
\label{sec:arrangements}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:arrangements}
On standard $4\times4$ 2048, both of the following full-chain arrangements
are reachable in exactly $65{,}533$ moves, the minimum for their mass:
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
4&8&16&32\\
64&128&256&512\\
8192&4096&2048&1024\\
16384&32768&65536&131072
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}
4&8&16&32\\
512&256&128&64\\
1024&2048&4096&8192\\
65536&131072&32768&16384
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular, the 4 may occupy the corner opposite the 131072, and the
131072 need not occupy a corner. The constructions and square symmetries
give at least $128$ distinct full-chain arrangements at this minimum
length, and realise the largest tile in each of the twelve boundary cells.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof by explicit certificates]
For the first family, retain the first $65{,}517$ moves of the existing
full-chain witness and enumerate all legal sixteen-move continuations
with 4-spawns. The search visits $199$ states, including its starting
state, and gives eight final arrangements. For the second family, retain
the first $49{,}166$ moves, append the explicit seventy-move bridge in
\path{research/noncorner-splice.json}, and resume the reflected suffix
at move $49{,}236$. At the splice the small tiles agree exactly; the
already completed 65536 and 131072 have exchanged labels relative to
the reflected reference. No subsequent merge touches these two tiles.

Both independent rule replayers check all sixteen reconstructed base
trajectories, including their exact final arrangements.
The script \path{research/spliced_certificates.py} checks the results
and counts $128$ distinct images under the eight square symmetries.
Legality of the images follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:equiv}.
Every spawn, including the opening, is a 4. The final mass is
$262140$, so the ledger fixes the move count to $(262140-8)/4=65533$.
\end{proof}

The two displayed representatives are published as
\path{witness/full-chain-opposite-corners.txt} and
\path{witness/full-chain-noncorner.txt}. These settle the two affirmative
existence questions in the earlier draft, not the complete classification
of full-chain arrangements. In particular, a full-chain placement of the
largest tile in one of the four interior cells is not established here.

\paragraph{Why suffix transplantation works.}
More generally, fix a legal suffix and a threshold $K$ such that every
merge output in the suffix is at most $K$. Distinct tiles larger than $K$
are inert. Permuting their labels, while leaving the small tiles and
occupied cells unchanged, preserves every slide, spawn vacancy, and
nontriviality check in that suffix. Indeed, decisions to merge depend on
equality, not numerical ordering: the large labels remain distinct, and
none can equal a small tile. Induction over the suffix proves the claim.
This is a reuse principle for checked trajectories, not a substitute for
establishing reachability of the new starting configuration.

\subsection{Terminal mass barriers}
\label{sec:barriers}

Write $m=\mass(B)/2$, so each spawned tile increases $m$ by 1 or 2, and
let $s_2(m)$ be the number of ones in its binary expansion. Throughout
this subsection $c\ge2$ and the opening consists of two ordinary tiles.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:barrier}
A board of $c$ cells whose mass in units of 2 satisfies $s_2(m)=c$ is full,
has pairwise distinct tiles, and admits no legal move.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any representation of $m$ as a sum of powers of two has at least $s_2(m)$
terms. Combining equal terms until the binary representation is reached
reduces the term count at each combination. With at most $c=s_2(m)$
terms initially, no such combination can occur. Thus there are exactly
$c$ distinct terms. On a full board without equal tiles a slide cannot
move anything. This argument uses arithmetic carries only to analyse a
representation; it does not assume those carries are legal board moves.
\end{proof}

Put
\[
H=2^{c+1}-2,\qquad b_j=H+1-2^j\quad(1\le j\le c),\qquad
A_c(m)=\#\{j:b_j<m\}.
\]
The values $b_c<b_{c-1}<\cdots<b_1<H$ all have $c$ binary ones.
Both $H-1$ and $H$ are terminal barriers. A first crossing above $H$
would have to start at one of these two values, so no play can exceed
mass $2H$. Each $b_j$ below the attained mass must be skipped, not visited.
A skip is possible only by a 4-spawn from $b_j-1$ to $b_j+1$. Distinct
barriers require distinct skips. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrierfours}
\nfour\ge A_c(m).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A second proof of the score and length ceilings.}
Let
\[
F(m)=\sum_{r\ge0:\,\text{bit}_r(m)=1}r\,2^{r+1}
\]
be the potential of the binary representation of the mass. Arithmetic
merging can only increase potential, so $\Phichain(B)\le F(m)$, with
equality precisely when the tile multiset is that binary representation.
If $r$ is the number of trailing ones in $m$, binary carrying gives
\[
F(m+1)-F(m)=4(2^r-1).
\]
The function $A_c$ increases only from $b_j$ to $b_j+1$, where $r=j\ge1$.
Thus $F(m)-4A_c(m)$ is nondecreasing on $0\le m\le H$. By the score
identity and \eqref{eq:barrierfours},
\[
\mathrm{score}\le F(m)-4A_c(m)\le F(H)-4c=\Phi_c-4c.
\]
Likewise $m-A_c(m)$ is nondecreasing, and the mass ledger gives
\[
T=m-\nfour-2\le m-A_c(m)-2\le H-c-2=2^{c+1}-c-4.
\]
This proves the two ceilings without merge-tree scheduling. It does not
prove geometric attainability of either ceiling on an arbitrary board.

\paragraph{Correct equality cases.}
The score envelope attains its maximum only at $m\in\{H-2,H-1,H\}$.
Equality requires the binary representation and $\nfour=A_c(m)$.
The binary representation at $H-2$ has no tile of value 2 or 4, so it
cannot be a completed round: the latest spawn is still present as such.
Hence the score ceiling can occur after a round only on the full chain,
with $c$ 4-spawns, or on that chain with its 4 replaced by a 2, with
$c-1$ 4-spawns. Both have $H-c-2$ moves. The earlier draft's full-chain-only
score equality clause missed the second case. A separately enumerated
$2\times2$ counterexample scores 180 in 24 moves and ends at
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}32&8\\16&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$; its complete
trajectory is in \path{research/2x2-score-equality-counterexample.txt}.

\subsection{A fixed spawn word for the maximum-score conjecture}
\label{sec:spawnword}

If the full chain is reached with exactly $c$ 4-spawns, every such spawn
must pay one of the $c$ skips in \eqref{eq:barrierfours}. No other 4-spawn
is available. This determines the entire sequence of spawn values,
including the two initial 2s: the 4-spawn skipping $b_j$ occurs at round
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forcedround}
t_j=H-2^j-(c-j)-1,\qquad j=c,c-1,\ldots,1.
\end{equation}
Indeed, before that spawn the mass in units of 2 is $H-2^j$, and $c-j$
of the previous spawns were 4s; the number of previous spawns is therefore
$H-2^j-(c-j)$. Subtracting the two opening spawns gives \eqref{eq:forcedround}.
For $4\times4$ the sixteen rounds are
\begin{align*}
&65533,\ 98300,\ 114683,\ 122874,\ 126969,\ 129016,\ 130039,\ 130550,\\
&130805,\ 130932,\ 130995,\ 131026,\ 131041,\ 131048,\ 131051,\ 131052.
\end{align*}
Every other spawn is a 2. Thus Conjecture~\ref{conj} is equivalent to
finding \emph{any legal placement-and-direction realisation of this one
fixed value word}. A legal realisation has mass $2H$ and $c$ 4-spawns,
and therefore automatically attains the score and length ceilings.
Conversely, a ceiling-scoring game ending with a 2 can have only that
last spawn changed to a 4. This is an exact reduction of the remaining
geometric task, not its solution.

\subsection{Policy-independent probability bounds}
\label{sec:massprob}

Let a spawn be a 4 with probability $p$ and a 2 with probability $q=1-p$,
independently, including the opening. The bounds below concern \emph{one}
ordinary game, with no undo or restarts, under any policy for choosing
moves. Pre-sample an infinite sequence of spawn values. Geometry may
stop play earlier but cannot let it cross a visited terminal mass barrier.

For a random walk with increments 1 and 2 of probabilities $q$ and $p$,
the probability $u_d$ of ever visiting $d$, starting at 0, satisfies
$u_0=1$, $u_1=q$, and $u_d=qu_{d-1}+pu_{d-2}$. Solving gives
\[
u_d=\frac{1-(-p)^{d+1}}{1+p}.
\]
Skipping the first barrier $b_c=2^c-1$ has probability $p u_{2^c-2}$.
After a skip the next necessary pre-skip mass is $2^j-2$ units farther
on, for $j=c-1,\ldots,1$. Independence of future increments yields:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:massprob}
For every move policy on a $c$-cell board,
\begin{align}
\Pr(\text{reach }2^{c+1})
 &\le \frac{p}{1+p}\bigl(1+p^{2^c-1}\bigr),\label{eq:tileprob}\\
\Pr(\text{reach the full chain})
 &\le \prod_{j=1}^{c}\frac{p}{1+p}\bigl(1+p^{2^j-1}\bigr).
 \label{eq:chainprob}
\end{align}
These are exact crossing probabilities for the mass-only relaxation,
not exact optimal probabilities for geometric 2048.
\end{theorem}

For the ordinary $4\times4$ game, \eqref{eq:tileprob} is
$(1+10^{-65535})/11<0.09091$, and \eqref{eq:chainprob} is approximately
$2.396314654\cdot10^{-17}$. The tiny positive correction in
\eqref{eq:tileprob} must not be dropped when stating a strict upper bound.
These bounds leave a wide gap to the probability of a particular witness.

\paragraph{Tracking the number of 4-spawns.}
Introduce the polynomial
\[
P_d(z)=\sum_{f=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}
 \binom{d-f}{f}q^{d-2f}(pz)^f,
\qquad
G_c(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{c} pz\,P_{2^j-2}(z).
\]
A composition of $d$ into increments 1 and 2 with $f$ size-2 increments has $d-f$
increments and can be ordered in $\binom{d-f}{f}$ ways. Hence
$[z^f]G_c(z)$ is the exact mass-only crossing probability with $f$
4-spawns, and an upper bound for a geometric full-chain play with that
count. This supplies a joint score--length probability envelope because
on the chain $\mathrm{score}=\Phi_c-4f$ and $T=H-f-2$.
The coefficient of $z^c$ is
$p^c q^{2^{c+1}-2c-2}$. Allowing either final spawn gives the necessary
value-word probability
$p^{c-1}q^{2^{c+1}-2c-2}$ for score-ceiling attainment.
The latter expression, the score formula, and the $131052$-move figure
already appear in Rip\`a's 2014 analysis \cite{ripa2014,ripaoeis2014};
we do not claim those formulas as new. Here their role is a necessary,
policy-independent bound, not an assumption that the needed geometry
can always be realised. The polynomial calculation is checked against
an independent exact rational dynamic program for $2\le c\le6$ by
\path{research/mass_barriers.py}.

\subsection{A computable upper bound on optimal expected score}
\label{sec:massbellman}

There is also a Bellman relaxation with only $H+1$ scalar mass states.
Stop the increment walk at its first mass with $c$ binary ones. Define
\[
W(m)=
\begin{cases}
F(m),&s_2(m)=c,\\
qW(m+1)+p\bigl(W(m+2)-4\bigr),&s_2(m)<c,
\end{cases}
\qquad 0\le m\le H.
\]
All successors used in the second case are at most $H$. This backward
recurrence exactly computes the expected terminal binary potential minus
four times the number of future 4-spawns in the relaxation.

To justify it as an upper bound, at spawn index $n$ let $m_n$ be the
mass and $f_n$ the number of 4-spawns, and put $R_n=F(m_n)-4f_n$.
The actual score is at most $R_n$. Moreover $R_n$ is nondecreasing:
a 2-spawn changes it by $F(m+1)-F(m)\ge0$, and a 4-spawn changes it by
$F(m+2)-F(m)-4\ge0$. The real game cannot continue beyond the walk's
first terminal barrier. Extending the pre-sampled walk after an earlier
geometric loss therefore still gives an upper bound. In particular,
for an ordinary random opening,
\[
\sup_\pi\mathbb E_\pi[\mathrm{score}]\le W(0).
\]
From an already given board $B$ of mass $m$ in units of 2, the analogous
upper bound on future score is $W(m)-\Phichain(B)$.
A second recurrence $J(m)=1+qJ(m+1)+pJ(m+2)$, with zero boundary values,
bounds the expected game length by $J(0)-2$.

For $c=16$, outward-rounded decimal evaluation gives
\begin{align*}
\sup_\pi\mathbb E_\pi[\mathrm{score}]&<1{,}915{,}854.958,\\
\sup_\pi\mathbb E_\pi[T]&<62{,}412.330.
\end{align*}
For comparison the four-cell score relaxation is $69.814912\ldots$,
above the exact geometric $2\times2$ value in Table~\ref{tab:honest}.
These are bounds, not strong solutions. The script
\path{research/mass_interval.py} uses fifty-digit arithmetic rounded
outwards at every operation and records enclosing intervals; a separate
forward computation checks the values numerically.

\subsection{Near-optimum rigidity and deadline probabilities}
\label{sec:slack}

If a $4\times4$ play reaches 131072 at round $T=32781+d$, inspect its
prefix ending fifteen moves earlier. The threshold-mass lemma requires
at least 131072 mass there. If $z$ is the number of 2-spawns in that
prefix, excluding the opening, and $M_0$ is the opening mass, the ledger
therefore gives
\[
2z+(8-M_0)\le4d.
\]
The left side also counts the opening 2s, at a charge of two mass units
each. Thus among the first $32768+d$ spawns, including the opening,
there can be at most $2d$ twos. The deterministic prefix inequality,
its general-target version, and the zero-slack consequence are
kernel-checked in \texttt{Game2048/DeadlineSlack.lean}.

Pre-sampling values again gives a bound for \emph{any} policy reaching
the tile by the deadline:
\[
\Pr_\pi(T_{131072}\le32781+d)
\le \sum_{z=0}^{\min(2d,32768+d)}
 \binom{32768+d}{z}(0.9)^z(0.1)^{32768+d-z}.
\]
For an earlier hit the required mass has already been acquired by the
specified prefix, so the same necessary event applies. At $d=0$ this
is $10^{-32768}$. For deadlines $40000$, $50000$, and $55000$, the
base-ten logarithms of the upper bounds are approximately
$-14854.03$, $-3668.61$, and $-909.55$. These are spawn-value bounds;
requiring suitable locations can only decrease the success probability.
The stochastic corollary is not separately formalised in Lean.
