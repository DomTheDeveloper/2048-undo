\subsection{Kernel-checked reachability and optimality in Lean}\label{sec:lean}

The independent replayers in Section~\ref{sec:certificate} check a concrete
trajectory. We additionally formalise its legality and its minimum length
in Lean~4 \cite{demoura2021lean4}. The development in
\texttt{lean-kernel/} uses Lean~4.19.0 and its standard library, without
Mathlib. The lower bound is proved directly for the same slide-and-spawn
semantics used to check the witness; no connection to an abstract
merge-tree model is assumed.

\paragraph{Formal rules and quantifiers.}
A \texttt{Board} has four rows of four natural-number values, and each
coordinate type has exactly four constructors. A row slide removes zeros,
merges equal adjacent inputs from the moving edge, and pads with zeros.
The evaluator is proved to satisfy a separate declarative
\texttt{PairMerge} relation: a newly produced tile is an output, not an
input to another merge in that slide. The row-length lemma ensures that
padding discards no tile. Reversal and transposition implement all four
whole-board directions. Separate lemmas prove exact mass conservation
under a slide and exact mass increase by the spawned value under a round.

The function \texttt{step} accepts a direction and a spawn only if the
slide changes the board, the spawn cell is empty after the slide, and the
spawn value is 2 or 4. One tile is then inserted. The inductive relation
\texttt{Plays}\,$a\,T\,b$ means exactly $T$ such rounds from $a$ to $b$.
The predicate \texttt{IsInitial} allows any two distinct cells and either
permitted value in each. There is no undo, restart, deletion, selective
row move, or arbitrary board edit in this relation. As elsewhere in this
paper, continuation after obtaining the 2048 tile is allowed, and the
count includes the mandatory spawn after the final slide.

\begin{theorem}[Lean-certified exact move counts]\label{thm:leanexact}
For standard $4\times4$ 2048 under possible play:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item Some ordinary two-tile opening admits a legal trajectory to
$131072$ of length $32{,}781$, and every trajectory to $131072$ from
any ordinary opening has length at least $32{,}781$.
\item When both opening tiles are required to be 2s, some such opening
admits a trajectory of length $32{,}782$, and every trajectory to
$131072$ from any such opening has length at least $32{,}782$.
\end{enumerate}
These are minima over permitted openings and legal spawn sequences, not
a claim that every initial placement attains the same minimum or that a
player can force those spawns.
\end{theorem}

The named Lean theorems are:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
Game2048.standard2048\_exact\_131072\\
Game2048.standard2048\_two\_twos\_exact
\end{quote}
Their statements conjoin an
existential legal trajectory with a universal lower bound over all legal
trajectories, rather than asserting only that a replay program returned
\texttt{true}.

\paragraph{The lower bound without ancestry bookkeeping.}
For a board $B$ and a nonnegative threshold $k$, put
\[
 H_k(B)=\sum_{x:\,B(x)>k}B(x).
\]
For each slide $S$, the formal development proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leanheavy}
 H_{2k}(S(B))\le H_k(B).
\end{equation}
An unmerged tile counted on the left was already counted on the right.
A merged tile $v+v$ exceeds $2k$ precisely when each input exceeds $k$.
No input can participate in another merge during that slide. For $k\ge2$
a new 2 or 4 contributes nothing above $2k$, so
\eqref{eq:leanheavy} also holds for a complete round.

Define $\operatorname{cutoff}(j)=2^{j+1}$. Iteration over the last $j$
rounds of a trajectory of length $T\ge j$ gives
\[
 H_{\operatorname{cutoff}(j)}(B_T)
 \le H_2(B_{T-j})
 \le \mass(B_{T-j})
 \le M_0+4(T-j).
\]
If $B_T$ contains $131072$, take $j=15$, whose cutoff is $65536$.
With $M_0\le8$ this gives $T\ge32{,}781$; with two initial 2s,
$M_0\le4$ gives $T\ge32{,}782$. The Lean proof handles $T<15$ as
well: iteration through all $T$ rounds bounds
$H_{\operatorname{cutoff}(T)}(B_T)$ by $H_2(B_0)\le8$, contradicting
the target tile. The general theorem \path{Plays.target_deadline}
expresses the resulting target-versus-time inequality. The development
also proves the power-of-two lower bound on the fixed $4\times4$ board
for ranks at least four and the $519$-round lower bound for 2048.
It does not assert attainment for arbitrary target sizes or board shapes.

\paragraph{The constructive half.}
The input is the unchanged plaintext \texttt{witness/131072.txt}.
An untrusted Python generator proposes the intermediate boards and emits
32,781 individual transition proofs using \texttt{decide +kernel}.
Each proof checks the actual \texttt{step} function, including nontrivial
movement and spawn legality. The resulting plays are composed into
258 blocks in 17 generated modules, then into a single trajectory.
The kernel checks both the ordinary opening and the occurrence of 131072
in the terminal board. Thus correctness of the Python generator, the AI,
and the JavaScript engine is not an assumption of the Lean theorem.

For the two-2s case, put the two 2s at $(3,2)$ and $(3,3)$, with
zero-based coordinates. One right slide merges them at $(3,3)$, followed
by a 4-spawn at $(3,2)$. The resulting board is exactly the two-4s opening
of the existing certificate. Lean verifies this extra round and composes
it with the original play. The derived plaintext witness is
\texttt{witness/131072-two-twos.txt}. The first construction, together
with the direct lower bound, proves part~(a) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:leanexact}; the one-round extension and the stronger
opening-mass bound prove part~(b).

The two-2s exact theorem likewise uses only
\texttt{propext} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. These are standard foundational
axioms of Lean, not hypotheses about 2048. The build rejects unfinished
proofs and nonstandard axioms, and it does not use native evaluation as a
proof oracle. Kernel reduction, the axiom audit, and review of the
formal statement serve different purposes \cite{leanvalidation2026}.
Regression examples test non-chaining merges and invalid spawns; a
negative test requires the kernel to reject a fabricated legal move on an
empty board. The source hash identifies the witness but is not a
substitute for checking its transitions.

\paragraph{Scope of the formal claim.}
The Lean result is the fixed-$4\times4$ reachability and exact minimum
round count, together with the supporting rule and mass lemmas just
described. It does not formalise the paper's largest-tile upper bound,
score ceiling, longest-game bound, primed-board classification, symmetry
orbit, general-dimensional claims, or optimal stochastic policy.
In particular, neither failure of the naive snake strategy nor success
of this certificate proves or disproves general rectangular-board
attainability. Every prescribed finite spawn sequence has positive
probability under the usual random rules, but the probability measure
itself is not formalised here. As in any proof-assistant development,
trust in the logic and kernel and review that the formal definitions
express the intended game remain separate from execution of the searcher.
We make no unconditional historical-priority claim for this formalisation.
